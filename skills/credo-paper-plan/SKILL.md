# Paper Plan

Produce an argument the author can draft from, or critique a draft against its intended argument. Adapted from the writing sections of Carlini's research essay; background episodes live in the credo-taste package. Read an episode only when using it.

Use existing evidence and decisions. Ask one consequential missing question at a time when the user wants an interview; otherwise propose a plan with unresolved choices clearly identified. Do not require another interview for information already available.

## Plan the argument

- **Idea and reader:** identify the contribution and the reader's starting understanding. Multiple related results can support one argument; an “and” is not by itself evidence of two papers.
- **Conclusion:** explain what the evidence would allow the reader to learn or do. Separate a hoped-for conclusion from what has actually been established.
- **Introduction:** connect the reader's problem to the question and contribution. Length follows the venue, reader, and argument; there is no universal two-page ceiling.
- **Figures:** storyboard the comparisons or relationships that carry the argument. A caption should support its intended reading context; do not require a figure to repeat the entire methods section.
- **Sections:** give each a purpose and explain the order. Omit a section that does no work for the argument.
- **Abstract:** state the problem, approach or result, and significance at the evidence's strength. Include a specific number when it conveys a supported result; do not invent or force one.
- **Unresolved work:** identify evidence or author decisions that would change the outline. Route an empirical gap to a bounded experiment rather than pretending a prose edit settles it.

Paper planning applies to short or long research efforts and near-term deadlines. It does not require a quarter-long horizon, publication novelty for every applied result, or a predetermined successful conclusion.

## Critique and deliver

Read a draft against the intended reader and argument. Identify concrete mismatches and their effect; preserve claims and evidence strength. A critique can recommend changes without rewriting unrequested prose.

Return the useful outline, storyboard, or critique. Stop at planning when that is the request. If the user also asks for drafting, continue within that authorization, using `copydesk` when available for production; the skill's ownership of planning does not reserve all prose for the human. Submission checks belong to `credo-release`.
